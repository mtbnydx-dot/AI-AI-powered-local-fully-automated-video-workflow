# Task and goal

* Write the assigned episode as a complete shot script for video generation. Use the supplied source, season bible, episode plan, editor_notes and brief.
* Attract short-drama viewers through substantial dialogue, inner monologue, character conflict and action. Turn narrative exposition into words and behavior the audience can hear and see.
* Preserve the episode ID, required events, causal developments and planned start/end states.

# Shots and dialogue

* Choose shot counts, durations, framing and camera movement from the scene's needs. Use positive durations within brief.maximum_requested_duration_seconds and the canvas in brief.width/height.
* Describe what happens within each shot and how it connects to the next. Dialogue exchanges, interruptions, reactions, ongoing actions and internal edits can serve the scene; describe their order clearly.
* action_start_zh and action_end_zh describe the actual states at the start and cut. action_complete records whether the main action has finished, as a boolean. cut_reason_zh explains the cut.
* dialogue lists actual spoken lines in order, including multiple speakers and turns when appropriate. text_zh contains the exact words. language uses an English language name, defaulting to brief.dialogue_language. delivery_en describes natural performance and pace for the conflict.
* On-screen speech uses voiceover=false and a registered visible speaker. Inner monologue and off-screen speech use voiceover=true; explain who can hear it and the corresponding visible performance.
* soundscape_en describes ambient sound and foley. Music follows brief.non_diegetic_music, with optional non_diegetic_music_en for a shot-specific arrangement.

# Picture and continuity

* first_frame_prompt_en describes the starting image in a self-contained English paragraph: framing, cast, wardrobe colors, positions, relevant objects, environment and light. visual_en describes the subsequent performance; camera_en describes the camera and any intended transitions.
* Track character knowledge, relationships, space and key-object states across cuts. Choose reactions and pauses for their role in the scene.
* Use registered IDs for featured characters, wardrobe, locations and key props. Describe background crowds as required by the story. reference_priority orders character, location or prop IDs by their importance to this image; image compilation selects references using the service configuration.
* acceptance_en lists story-critical observable requirements and may be empty. Story-relevant readable text is allowed; overlays follow brief.on_screen_text and editor_notes.

# Output

* Return one JSON object using this structure. Narrative fields are Chinese; _en fields are English descriptions and may quote original story text. Dialogue uses its specified language.
* Shots are in viewing order with unique IDs formed from the episode ID and _s01, _s02 onward. scene_id identifies each continuous location/time block; time_of_day uses dawn, morning, afternoon, dusk, evening or night.
* characters, prop_ids, dialogue, reference_priority and acceptance_en may be empty. The example illustrates field types.
